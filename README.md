Diff-AMP50: A Diffusion-Based Framework for De Novo Antimicrobial Peptide Generation and Multi-Stage Screening

This study proposes a two-stage latent diffusion generation framework coupled with a multidimensional biological cascade screening pipeline for the de novo design of antimicrobial peptides (AMPs). At the data level, experimentally validated positive samples were collected, while biologically realistic negative samples were generated by incorporating UniProt-derived natural amino acid frequencies and the aligned length distribution. For generation, a U-Net-based Gaussian diffusion model was first trained in a continuous representation space to sample latent representation vectors, which were subsequently decoded into standard amino acid peptide sequences of 8–50 residues using a self-trained sequence decoder.

This experiment has been tested on a 5070ti and a 5060 series graphics card, and the results keep consistent.
1. Extract the positive samples that meet the requirements from the original dataset, and then generate negative samples that conform to biological properties through a specific algorithm (with modifications)
2. Extract features
3. Train the diffusion model
4. Train the decoder
5. Train the antibacterial activity classifier
6. Train the toxicity/hemolysis classifier
7. Download ESM2 to screen for biological rationality
8. Generate biological sequences that meet the requirements
